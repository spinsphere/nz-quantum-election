# The Opportunity Party (TOP) - 2026 policy research

Research date: 2026-10-06. Election: 7 November 2026.
Method note: WebSearch quota was exhausted, and the party site blocked repeated fetches. Findings come from the party's own policy pages (WebFetch summaries of each page) and Wikipedia. Few independent news or analyst sources could be retrieved, so "Independent assessment" bullets are mostly UNVERIFIED or flagged as background knowledge. Party-site pages are party claims, not verified outcomes. The old domain top.org.nz 301-redirects to https://www.opportunity.org.nz/ (party now brands as "Opportunity Party").

## Party snapshot

- Leader: Qiulae (Q) Wong, standing in Mt Albert. She became leader in November 2025, replacing Raf Manji (who had stepped down as leader in December 2023, per Wikipedia). Background: former KPMG sustainability adviser, B Lab NZ director. https://www.opportunity.org.nz/ ; https://opportunity.org.nz/team ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_Party_(New_Zealand)
- Deputy: Daniel Eb (Kaipara ki Mahurangi). https://opportunity.org.nz/team
- Other candidates (party site): Johan Chang (Auckland Central), Jamie Attenborough (Mt Roskill), Kevin Huang (Pakuranga), Holly Knill (North Shore), Kayla Kingdon-Bebb (Wellington Bays), Jessica Hammond (Wellington North), Chee-Khiang Sng (Kapiti), Eden Skipper (Christchurch Central), Dave Bainbridge-Zafar (Dunedin), Bianca Beebe (Southland), Michael Carter (Tauranga), Naomi Pocock (Hamilton East), Netra Ghimire (Palmerston North). https://opportunity.org.nz/team
- Electorates: plans to contest about 30 electorates (Wikipedia). Ilam: Raf Manji came second there in 2023 (Wikipedia), but Manji is no longer leader and no evidence found that Ilam is a 2026 target. Mt Albert (Wong) is a safe-ish Labour seat historically; the party's chance of winning any electorate is UNVERIFIED and likely low. The realistic route is the 5% party-vote threshold. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_Party_(New_Zealand)
- Polling (via Wikipedia polling page): 1News-Verian 23-27 Sep 2026: 6%. The Post/Freshwater Strategy 4-11 Sep 2026: 6%. Reported range: 9.5% in one Roy Morgan poll, 4.5% in a September Taxpayers' Union poll. So the party is hovering around the 5% threshold, within poll error. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2026_New_Zealand_general_election ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_Party_(New_Zealand)
- Past results: 2017 2.4%, 2020 1.5%, 2023 2.2% (no seats). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_Party_(New_Zealand)
- Funding: $491,660 donations in 2026 to 24 Sep (donors incl. Phillip Mills $50,000, Brian Cartmell $100,000); broadcasting allocation about $118,755. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election
- Coalition stance: Luxon ruled out working with Opportunity, including confidence and supply (27 July and 24 August), citing its tax policy. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election . Party reported as open to Labour or National depending on who gets the most votes, and reported as ruling out working with Winston Peters as foreign minister (UNVERIFIED beyond Wikipedia). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_Party_(New_Zealand)
- Five bottom lines (https://opportunity.org.nz/bottom_lines):
  1. Bring prices down: empower the Commerce Commission to investigate and force structural separation of anti-competitive businesses.
  2. Clean up politics: mandatory lobbyist register and code of conduct; per-donor donation cap, registered voters only.
  3. Fix the health system: decade-long cross-party health strategy, funding for health workers, prevention focus.
  4. Revive ocean health: immediately stop bottom trawling in damaged inshore areas (esp. Hauraki Gulf/Tikapa Moana), phase out nationally.
  5. Restore climate credibility: recommit to Paris Agreement obligations; protect the Climate Change Commission.
  Party also says honouring Te Tiriti stays central to coalition talks. Note: the land value tax / citizen's income is NOT listed as a bottom line.

## 1. Health and Mental Health

- 10-year cross-party health plan; raise health spend from 7.4% of GDP to 9% over a decade; more investment in primary care clinics. https://opportunity.org.nz/healthy_people
- Prevention: tobacco, alcohol and unhealthy food cited as a quarter of healthy years lost; restore school lunches; pursue Smokefree NZ. https://opportunity.org.nz/healthy_people
- Political reform angle: lobbying register, cooling-off periods, limits on tobacco-industry influence. https://opportunity.org.nz/healthy_people
- Maori health: stable multi-year funding for Maori and iwi-led providers, measurable equity goals. https://opportunity.org.nz/healthy_people
- Mental health, Pharmac, dental: no specific policy found on the page summary (UNVERIFIED; full PDF not retrieved).
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED (none retrieved).

## 2. Education

- Tertiary: forgive student loan interest for returning citizens; convert polytechnics into regional innovation hubs. https://www.opportunity.org.nz/breakthrough-economy

## 3. Crime and Justice

- "Smart on Crime": evidence-based; every justice initiative designed by experts and rigorously evaluated. https://opportunity.org.nz/smart_on_crime
- Prevention: early intervention (mentoring, parenting courses, trauma counselling); specialist courts (Young Adult List, Te Pae Oranga, Alcohol and Other Drug Treatment Court); cannabis regulation to reduce youth incarceration.
- Victims: scale restorative justice; complete rollout of specialist sexual violence courts (pilot said to cut trial times about one third); review victims' rights.
- Reintegration: vocational training with employer partnerships; specialist post-release case management; mentoring. Cites over half of released prisoners reconvicted within two years.
- Gangs, sentencing, police numbers: UNVERIFIED, not found.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 4. Housing and Rental Costs

- Problem framing: average house about 7x median household income; 60% of under-40s not owners. https://opportunity.org.nz/affordable_housing
- Supply: cut 1,000+ planning zones to 10 national zones; pre-approval for prefab/modular; enable co-housing, papakainga, multi-generational homes. (This implies RMA-style planning reform; no detailed RMA position retrieved.)
- Quality: extend Warmer Kiwi Homes to rentals, solar, electrification; refund GST to councils on qualifying new builds; raise Healthy Homes Standards; new builds to include EV charging and solar wiring.
- Finance: Kainga Ora to build region-specific mixes; low-cost government finance for councils, CHPs, iwi, developers; developers can defer land value tax up to two years during development.
- Land value tax (LVT): 1.75% annual on land value, 0.5% for rural/agricultural land; deferrals for asset-rich retirees; party claims it reduces property prices 10-15% and raises about $4bn/yr; phased over about a decade. https://opportunity.org.nz/tax-reset (https://www.opportunity.org.nz/tax-reset). Exact base (e.g. owner-occupied homes, family home treatment, thresholds) UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment (background knowledge, UNVERIFIED for 2026 policy specifically): the 2019 Tax Working Group did not recommend a broad residential LVT, flagging liquidity problems for asset-rich/cash-poor owners and valuation and transition issues; it recommended extending taxes on capital gains instead. Economists generally regard land taxes as efficient and hard to avoid. No 2026 costing by Treasury, NZIER, Motu or Infometrics retrieved. Please verify.

## 5. Cost of Living and Economy

- Tax Reset (https://www.opportunity.org.nz/tax-reset): Citizen's Income up to $370/week ($19,400/yr) for most adults 18+, replacing most existing benefits; LVT as above; KiwiSaver 2.0 compulsory with employee/employer rates rising to 6% over time. Party claims: 70% of New Zealanders better off, 20% unchanged; 50,000 families lifted out of poverty; admin savings over $1.7bn; "fully funded"; phased by age cohort over about a decade. Renters and lower earners gain most, homeowners break even or small gains. Income tax cut / tax-free threshold detail: UNVERIFIED (page mentions income tax cuts, no threshold retrieved).
- Breakthrough Economy (https://www.opportunity.org.nz/breakthrough-economy): R&D to 2% of GDP; $600m technology adoption tax credit; $175m contestable funds; double accelerator funding; "Impact Company" legal form; total about $1.33bn extra annual operating cost; claims 2% extra annual GDP growth after ten years (party estimate).
- Competition: Commerce Commission power to break up monopolies/structural separation (supermarkets, banks); party claims concentration costs families about $30/week. Also a bottom line.
- Clean Up Politics (https://www.opportunity.org.nz/clean_up_politics): mandatory lobbying register; one-year ministerial cooling-off; statutory code of conduct with fines/deregistration; $30,000 per-donor cap per cycle, registered NZ voters only (no corporate or trust donations); disclosure threshold $1,000; independent anti-corruption body; ban deceptive election ads; stronger OIA and whistleblower protection; independent policy costing unit; cost about $52m/yr ($40m anti-corruption body, $10m Electoral Commission, $2m costing).
- Infrastructure Fund: $60bn over ten years at Crown borrowing rates (see section 7). Debt: party cites NZ government debt about 50% of GDP, as room for targeted borrowing.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED. Note the party's own $4bn LVT revenue claim looks small relative to the cost of a $19,400 payment to most adults (adult population about 4m); the full-cost reconciliation (reallocation of benefits, NZ Super, tax changes) is not in the retrieved summary. Verify against the full policy paper and independent costings. Luxon cites tax policy as the reason for ruling out cooperation.

## 7. Transport and Infrastructure

- Abundant Energy (https://www.opportunity.org.nz/abundant-energy): 30GW renewable capacity target by 2050; Australian-style capacity investment scheme; ETS revenue funds industrial electrification; ringfence government power company profits (about $500m/yr) for reinvestment; merge five energy regulators into one; cut 29 distributors to at most eight; low-interest loans via Ratepayer Assistance Scheme; community energy and insulation; electrify public transport.
- Intergenerational Infrastructure (https://opportunity.org.nz/intergenerational_infrastructure): legislate the 30-year National Infrastructure Plan; parliamentary approval needed to cancel projects over $250m; scorecards; 60% of spend on maintenance and renewal (cites OECD ranking NZ last of 33 on upkeep); $60bn Infrastructure Fund over ten years at Crown rates; infrastructure bonds open to KiwiSaver investors.
- Climate Action (https://opportunity.org.nz/climate_action): low-interest loans and tax breaks to electrify homes, businesses and vehicle fleets (EVs); $1bn fund to move industry off coal and gas; $5bn Climate Resilience Fund ($500m nature-based); National Insurance Support Framework; carbon pricing reform; methane in ETS (Wikipedia). Bottom line: recommit to Paris, protect Climate Change Commission.
- Healthy Oceans: end bottom trawling in damaged inshore areas, phase out nationally (https://opportunity.org.nz/bottom_lines).
- Roads vs public transport specifics: UNVERIFIED (only "electrify public transport" found).
- Independent assessment: party cites OECD (maintenance ranking) and Te Waihanga (government accepted its 16 recommendations in June 2026, per party) - not independently verified.

## Update 6 Oct 2026 (full policy papers retrieved)
Sources: cdn.opportunity.org.nz policy PDFs (Tax Reset, Tax Reset Transition Plan, Healthy People, Affordable Housing, Smart on Crime) and the public pages supplied by the project owner.
- **Tax Reset costing (party's own, amended 7 Aug 2026):** Citizen's Income $19,400 a year to every adult; headline cost $69.6b, less income-tax clawback $23.4b and replaced benefits/NZ Super/student allowance $32.6b = net $13.6b; supplementary supports (sole parent $9,500, disability $6,000, superannuitant top-ups $10,000 single/$5,250 each for couples, housing support averaging $10,500 for families) net $8.3b; total cost $21.9b. Revenue: Land Value Tax 1.75% urban / 0.5% rural on an estimated $1.7 trillion land base = $24.3b, plus $1.7b administration savings (MSD $977m, IRD $255m) = $26.0b. Net fiscal position claimed +$4.1b a year. Income tax brackets become 28% to $50,000, 34% to $200,000, 39% above. Supports abate at 10c per dollar from household income of $50,000-$75,000. No independent costing located.
- **Transition plan:** two years of design, then Citizen's Income rolled out by age tranche (18-29 first) over four years; LVT starts at 0.5% urban only, reaching 1.75%/0.5% in year 6; KiwiSaver 2.0 contributions rise 0.5 points a year each to 6%+6%; party expects land values to fall 10-15% mostly through announcement effects; full implementation about a decade.
- **Healthy People:** legislated ten-year Health Plan reviewed mid-term; health spend 7.4% to 9% of GDP (independent estimate of the gap cited as ~1.5% of GDP); cost pressures funded in full via a published index (notes Budget 2026 funded $1.37b against a $1.405b independently assessed standstill cost); first-level care from 5.7% to 10% of Health NZ spending; legislated nutrition standards; National Nutrition Survey; smokefree pathway with de-nicotinisation; restore the pre-2024 school lunch model; lobbying register and cooling-off periods.
- **Affordable Housing:** 10 national planning zones; national pre-approval for prefab/modular; co-housing and papakāinga enabled; Warmer Kiwi Homes extended to rentals with double glazing/solar via a Ratepayer Assistance Scheme; GST refunded to councils on qualifying new builds; higher Healthy Homes Standards; performance-based Building Code; Kāinga Ora to build region-specific mixes; low-cost Crown finance for councils, CHPs, iwi and developers; two-year LVT deferral during development.
- **Smart on Crime:** voluntary early interventions from the first Oranga Tamariki Report of Concern; expand Young Adult List, Te Pae Oranga, AODT Court and Rākau Ora; regulate cannabis; scale restorative justice; complete specialist sexual violence court rollout (pilot cut time to trial by about a third); employer-linked prison training; specialist post-release case management and mentoring. Cites over half of released prisoners reconvicted within two years.
- **Bottom lines (confirmed):** ComCom structural-separation powers; lobbyist register and donation cap; ten-year cross-party health plan; end inshore bottom trawling then phase out; recommit to Paris and protect the Climate Change Commission. Te Tiriti partnership is "an approach we will bring to the negotiation table" but not a numbered bottom line.
- **Polling:** RNZ-Reid Research 24 Sep-1 Oct 2026: 5.5% (second consecutive RNZ poll above 5%); Taxpayers' Union-Curia 1-3 Sep: 4.5%. Seat projections flip to a National-ACT-NZ First majority when TOP falls below the threshold (Wikipedia projection table).
