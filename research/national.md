## 1. Health & Mental Health

### Stated policy / commitments
- Pledge: "faster healthcare and more medicines"; continue the five national health targets to 2030 (95% ED within 6 hours; 95% elective treatment and 95% first specialist assessment within 4 months; 90% cancer treatment within 31 days; 95% childhood immunisation at 24 months) — https://www.healthnz.govt.nz/about-us/what-we-do/planning-and-performance/health-targets
- Hospital funding: Budget 2026 described as a $5.5b–$5.8b increase for frontline health over four years incl. $1.37b per year cost-pressure uplift; capital for a 158-bed ward at Whangārei Hospital and land for a new South Auckland hospital; new hospitals under way in Nelson and Dunedin; 38 new/replacement CT, MRI and SPECT scanners over three years — https://budget.govt.nz/budget/2026/at-a-glance/health.htm ; https://thespinoff.co.nz/politics/28-05-2026/whats-in-tough-love-budget-2026-and-whats-missing ; https://www.national.org.nz/healthcare
- Pharmac: $604m over four years (announced June 2024) for up to 54 medicines; government says 66 additional medicines funded incl. 33 cancer medicines; Budget 2026 added $54m ($13.5m/yr) against a $1.76b 2025/26 Pharmac budget; 12-month prescriptions; more funded medicines via community pharmacies (Oct 2026) — https://www.national.org.nz/healthcare ; https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/515499/pharmac-funding-will-not-stretch-to-new-medicines-chief-executive
- Primary care/workforce: Waikato University medical school; ~2,000 extra nurses claimed; 120 nurse-practitioner training places by 2026; 24/7 digital health service (July 2025, 20,000+ consults); overseas-trained doctors into GP practices — https://www.national.org.nz/healthcare
- Prevention/screening: bowel screening age lowered to 56 (effective 30 Sep 2026; modelled 771 cancers and 556 deaths prevented over 25 years); breast screening extended to 70–74; free cervical screening 25–69; three-day postnatal stays — https://www.national.org.nz/healthcare
- Mental health: five mental health and addiction targets (set July 2024); $61.1m crisis-response boost (Nov 2025); Mental Health and Wellbeing Strategy (July 2026); workforce plan refresh (Sept 2026); new mental health unit Palmerston North; Gumboot Friday funding ($24m) **UNVERIFIED as to evaluation** — https://www.national.org.nz/news/20251105-hon-matt-doocey-delivering-a-better-mental-health-crisis-response ; https://www.national.org.nz/news/260924-mattdoocey

### Track record / outcome data
- Health targets Q4 2025/26 (Apr–Jun 2026), year-on-year: elective treatment <4 months 72.6% (from 63.9%); FSA <4 months 66.1% (from 62.0%); ED within 6 hours 76.3% (from 73.9%); cancer treatment within 31 days 87.0% (from 86.3%); immunisation at 24 months 83.7% (from 82.0%). Government reports 14,200 fewer people waiting >4 months for electives (−41%) and an 11% fall in the overall waitlist from baseline — https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2609/S00230/health-targets-five-out-of-five-moving-in-the-right-direction.htm . All targets remain below the 2030 goals (95%/90%).
- Mental health targets Q4 2025/26: 83%+ accessed primary mental health support within one week; 82%+ specialist within three weeks; 70.8% of mental-health ED presentations resolved within six hours (target 80%); youth: 77.5% primary within a week, 78.7% specialist within three weeks — https://www.national.org.nz/news/260924-mattdoocey
- Health NZ ran a $722m deficit in 2024/25 and said savings programmes would run to 2027 — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/535521/health-nz-says-cuts-will-keep-going-to-2027-reports-722m-deficit

### Independent assessment
- Health unions (ASMS, NZNO, PSA) analysis of Budget 2026 finds a $300–600m funding gap after removal of ~$300m "old money"; Hospital and Specialist Services line 1.2% lower; characterises it as "managed decline". Ministry of Health disputes this, saying demographic/price uplifts are built into baselines — https://newsroom.co.nz/2026/07/02/health-budget-shortfall-unions-warn-of-up-to-600m-funding-gap/ ; https://asms.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Health-Unions-Budget-Analysis-2026.pdf
- Public Health Communication Centre / VUW estimate $1.405b per year is needed just to "keep the lights on"; primary care received no significant increase in Budget 2026 — https://www.phcc.org.nz/briefing/insufficient-and-poorly-targeted-budget-means-health-system-will-continue-fall-short
- Pharmac's chief executive said the Budget 2026 top-up would not stretch to new medicines; Patient Voice Aotearoa and Breast Cancer Foundation say the waiting list of unfunded medicines is largely unchanged — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/politics/515499/pharmac-funding-will-not-stretch-to-new-medicines-chief-executive ; https://www.breastcancerfoundation.org.nz/news-articles/article/budget-2026-keeps-pharmac-afloat-but-leaves-breast-cancer-patients-waiting/1046
- Positive: official Health NZ quarterly data show sustained improvement on all five targets for two consecutive years; the elective wait-time gain (+8.7pp in a year) is the largest.

Area-school agreement rejected by NZEI members Feb 2026 — https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/education/580872/secondary-teachers-vote-to-accept-government-s-latest-collective-agreement-settlement ; https://workforce.education.govt.nz/principals-and-schools/collective-agreement-negotiations/primary-teachers-collective-agreement-negotiation ; https://workforce.education.govt.nz/principals-and-schools/collective-agreement-negotiations/area-school-teachers-collective-agreement-negotiation
- Early childhood: ECE regulatory review (Ministry for Regulation) — Cabinet agreed 15 changes; licensing criteria cut from 98 to 80 (from 20 Apr 2026); ECE regulation moved to ERO from 1 Sep 2026; ECE Funding Review (MAG chaired by Linda Meade) reporting late 2026; FamilyBoost ECE rebate (see Domain 5) — https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2504/S00186/ece-sector-review-changes-begin.htm ; https://www.education.govt.nz/our-work/strategies-policies-and-programmes/early-learning/ministerial-advisory-group-early-childhood-education-funding-system-review

- Gangs Act 2024 (patch ban in force 21 Nov 2024): public insignia ban (fine up to $5,000 / 6 months), dispersal notices, non-consorting orders, gang membership as aggravating factor — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangs_Act_2024
- Oranga Tamariki (Responding to Serious Youth Offending) Amendment Act passed Sept 2026 under urgency: Young Serious Offender declaration (14–17, two 10-year-plus offences proven), military-style academies as a court order — https://newswire.co.nz/2026/09/boot-camps-become-law-youth-offending-bill-urgency/ ; https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/PA2609/S00180/bill-continues-progress-on-responding-to-serious-youth-offending.htm
