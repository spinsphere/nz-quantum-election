# ACT New Zealand - 2026 Election Research (as of 2026-10-06)

**Method and limits:** The WebSearch budget for this session was exhausted (200/200), so only WebFetch was used. Sources are act.org.nz index/policy pages (which showed headings only, with sub-pages 404ing on guessed URLs) and Wikipedia pages. No NZ news outlet or independent-agency (Treasury, RBNZ, OECD, NZIER, ERO) documents could be fetched. Anything not directly seen in a fetched page is marked UNVERIFIED. Independent assessments are therefore thin and should be followed up.

## Leadership, campaign, coalition
- Leader: David Seymour (Epsom). Deputy leader: Nicole McKee (Wellington Bays) per act.org.nz (https://www.act.org.nz); Wikipedia states McKee appointed June 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_New_Zealand). Seymour was Deputy PM from 31 May 2025 (same Wikipedia page).
- Campaign line: "Party Vote ACT to re-elect the Coalition and help it go further and faster" (https://www.act.org.nz).
- Oct 2026 announcements listed on homepage: parent visa support; no tax on KiwiSaver earnings; Seymour economic speech on wealth creation (https://www.act.org.nz). Policy pages dated 5 Oct 2026.
- Previously stated 15% party-vote target (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_New_Zealand). Currently 11 seats; ~$4.79m donations by Oct 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election).
- Coalition: National, ACT, NZ First. Nothing fetched on specific post-election coalition demands beyond the "go further and faster" line. Opposition parties have said they would repeal the Regulatory Standards Act (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_Standards_Bill).
- Policy areas on ACT's site: Economy & Cost of Living, Health, Law & Order, Equal Rights & Democracy, Housing & Infrastructure, Rural NZ, Education, Hunting/Conservation/Firearms.

## 1. Health & Mental Health
- Abolish Maori Health Authority; remove race-based provisions from health law; require measurable health targets; expand private provider opportunities (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/health). Wikipedia lists the Maori Health Authority as disestablished by this government (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_New_Zealand).
- Campaign initiatives (titles only; details not retrieved): "A Starship for Cancer", "Let Pharmacies Do More", "Strengthening End of Life Choice", "Closing the Medicines Gap" (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/health). Pharmacist empowerment to manage medical issues and reduce GP load (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election).
- Track record: Government-wide Pharmac funding of NZ$604m over four years for 54 medicines incl. 26 cancer drugs, announced 24 June 2024 by Health Minister Reti (not an ACT-specific policy) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).
- Mental health: no ACT-specific policy found. UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: none retrieved.

## 2. Education
- 2026 commitments: Affordable School Uniforms; Let the Kid on the Bus (rural buses passing with empty seats); Disrupt the Class, Leave the Class; Equal Treatment at Universities; Real Choice for Students; Keeping Children Safe (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/education). Details not retrieved.
- Charter schools: NZ$153m (Budget 2024) to convert 35 state schools and open 15 new; 7 opened Feb 2025, 5 in Term 1 2026, one more 20 Jul 2026; 18 operating as of July 2026; 1,732 students across 17 schools at 1 Jul 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_schools_in_New_Zealand).
- Structured literacy in all state schools from 2025 (National's Stanford, not ACT-specific) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).
- Teacher performance pay, NCEA, ECE regulation: no ACT position retrieved. UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: 2017 Martin Jenkins report of the earlier partnership schools praised most for helping Maori, Pacific and disadvantaged students; a 2012 Massey report said there was no international evidence they do better (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_schools_in_New_Zealand). No ERO evaluation of the 2025-26 schools retrieved.

## 3. Crime & Justice
- Three Strikes reinstated 25 Jun 2024 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand); 2026 policy to add burglary (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election). ACT site lists: Three Strikes for violent crime and burglary, victim protections, youth crime, ram raid prevention, rural crime, public space safety, deportation of serious offenders, pets in domestic violence (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/law-and-order). ACT claims violent crime, youth crime and ram raids "dropped sharply" (no figures given; UNVERIFIED).
- Gangs: patch ban in public and gathering restrictions announced 25 Feb 2024 (Wikipedia, Sixth National Government page above).
- Firearms: Arms Act 2026 replaced the Arms Act 1983 in late July 2026; most provisions in force 23 Sep 2026, the rest by Feb 2028 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_Act_1983). ACT (McKee) drove the rewrite. Thorp Report (1997) had recommended a rewrite and an independent Firearms Authority (same page). ACT opposed the 2019 semi-automatic ban.
- Prison capacity, rehabilitation: no ACT specifics retrieved. UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: none retrieved (Ministry of Justice/Sentencing Council evidence on Three Strikes deterrence not fetched).

## 4. Housing & Rental Costs
- Interest deductibility restored: 80% from 1 Apr 2024, 100% from Apr 2025; extra NZ$800m (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Government_of_New_Zealand).
- No-cause terminations restored (42 days' notice), pet bonds of up to two weeks' rent (same page).
- Housing & Infrastructure page: opened building-products market, rewards councils that consent homes, "Innovation Trials", "A Fair Contribution for Infrastructure" ($6/day surcharge on temporary work visa holders per Wikipedia 2026 election page) (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/infrastructure).
- RMA replacement committed by the coalition; ACT's Seymour leads regulation reform (Wikipedia, Sixth National Government page). Kainga Ora/social housing position: not retrieved. UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: none retrieved.

## 5. Cost of Living & Economy
- Site policies: No Tax on KiwiSaver Earnings; Open the Door to Supermarket Competition; Abundant and Affordable Electricity; Scrap Net Zero, Cut the Carbon Tax; A Smaller, More Affordable Government (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/economy). Detail/costings not retrieved. Income tax rates/thresholds, Reserve Bank mandate, fiscal rules: UNVERIFIED.
- Spending: reduce overall government spending, merge ministerial portfolios, consolidate diversity/cultural ministries into Ministry for Culture and Heritage (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election). Govt asked departments for NZ$1.5b annual savings (Jan 2024) (Sixth National Government page).
- "Split-gas" emissions target easing methane limits (2026 election page).
- Regulatory Standards Act 2025: in force 1 Jan 2026; board members named 20 Apr 2026; select committee recommended limiting compensation to severe cases and excluding Marine and Coastal Area legislation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_Standards_Bill).
- Independent assessment: Ministry for the Environment/Ministry of Justice-type RIS not fetched; Treasury "robustly criticised" a similar 2011 concept; ministry estimated 88% of ~23,000 submissions opposed, Seymour said 99.5% were bots (same Wikipedia page). Treasury view on the 2025 bill UNVERIFIED.

## 6. Employment
- Fair Pay Agreements Act repealed 14 Dec 2023; 90-day trials for all employers restored 21 Dec 2023 (Sixth National Government page). Minimum wage rose 2% to $23.15 (1 Apr 2024); ACT's own 2026 minimum wage position not retrieved. UNVERIFIED.
- Medical certificate requirements for beneficiaries; five-year welfare exclusion for new residency visa holders (2026 election page).
- Public service: at least 845 job losses signalled by 6 Apr 2024 (Sixth National Government page). Pay equity changes (2025): UNVERIFIED, not retrieved.
- Independent assessment: none retrieved.

## 8. Te Tiriti o Waitangi & Maori Issues
- Treaty Principles Bill: first reading passed 68-54 on 14 Nov 2024 (National, ACT, NZ First for); defeated 112-11 at second reading 10 Apr 2025, only ACT supporting (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_Principles_Bill). ~300,000 submissions, about 90% of written opposed.
- Independent assessment: Waitangi Tribunal (Aug 2024) found the Crown breached Treaty principles and recommended abandoning the bill; Ministry of Justice RIS said it "does not accurately reflect Article 2" (same page).
- Current platform: "Wind up the Waitangi Tribunal", "One Law for All", constitutional equal rights protections, merit-based public service, local referendums, property rights (https://www.act.org.nz/policies/democracy). No new 2026 Treaty Principles bill found. UNVERIFIED whether ACT proposes a new constitutional measure.
- Track record: Maori Health Authority and co-governance (Three Waters) scrapped; Maori wards binding referendums law passed 30 Jul 2024; English language legislated (Sixth National Government page).

## Update 6 Oct 2026
- ACT supermarket policy (28 Sep 2026): one-stop consenting for new supermarkets and default acceptance of overseas food labels; Seymour ruled out the other parties' interventionist (structural separation) policies.
- Energy policy (18 Sep): retain gentailers, market-based lines cost control, residential solar sold back at market rates. Also: 20 noise cameras in Tāmaki; Crown purchase of the former Epsom education campus for a school; ban on compulsory karakia in resource consents (4 Oct).
- ACT sub-page detail retrieved by crawl is recorded in research/raw (see manifest.json).
- Pass-2 crawl of act.org.nz sub-pages (6 Oct 2026): Closing the Medicines Gap (medicines 4.9% of health budget vs Australia 12.2%, OECD 13.3%; lift one point a year to parity by 2033, ~$320m more in a Budget-2026 year; record $6.294b four-year Pharmac budget, 133 funding decisions, 'Rule of Two' approvals, 12-month prescriptions); Let Pharmacies Do More (pharmacists manage stable hypertension, diabetes, cholesterol; skin-lesion checks; 120,000 used the Minor Health Conditions pilot, 88% needs met); Three Strikes for Burglary (three-year minimum, no parole); Disrupt the Class, Leave the Class (ERO 2024: half of teachers face disruption every lesson; mandatory stand-downs, parent fines up to $3,000); education record (16 charter schools, 1,471 students at 1 Mar 2026; regular attendance 64.2% Term 2 2026 vs 58.4%; school lunches $3.58 a meal vs up to $8.68); Backing Small Business (training wage 60% of adult minimum for under-20s in first year); A Fair Contribution for Infrastructure ($6/day surcharge on temporary work visas, ~$80m a year); infrastructure record (Incentives for Growth Fund $400m; Sustaining Tenancies framework removed; social housing waitlist down ~6,000 by May 2026); One Law for All (constrain judicial Treaty interpretation, Parliament to state Treaty meaning); A Starship for Cancer (remove $33,333 donation tax-benefit cap).
