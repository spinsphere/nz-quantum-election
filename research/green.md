# Green Party of Aotearoa NZ - 2026 Election Policy Research

Research date: 2026-10-06. Election: 7 Nov 2026.

**Method caveat:** The WebSearch budget was exhausted at the start of the session (0 searches succeeded). All findings come from WebFetch of greens.org.nz pages (summaries produced by a fetch-summariser model, not verbatim reads) and one Wikipedia page. No independent outlets (RNZ, Herald, Stuff, Treasury, NZIER, etc.) were retrieved, so "Independent assessment" is mostly UNVERIFIED. Treat figures as needing a second check.

## Leadership, coalition, recent announcements
- Co-leaders: Marama Davidson and Chlöe Swarbrick (Swarbrick since March 2024, succeeding James Shaw). No 2025-26 change found on the party homepage (https://www.greens.org.nz; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Aotearoa_New_Zealand). Davidson listed among 13 Māori candidates on the 2026 list (https://www.greens.org.nz/te_tiriti_o_waitangi).
- Coalition: In early Feb 2026 Labour and the Greens announced they would work together during the election and in a future government (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election). Specific rule-outs: UNVERIFIED.
- Announcements per https://www.greens.org.nz/news: 14 Sep $200m te reo Māori; 23 Sep cap political donations at $30,000 plus public lobbyist register; 25 Sep survey claims nearly two-thirds back wealth tax (party-cited; poll provider UNVERIFIED); 29 Sep criticised Pre-election Economic and Fiscal Update; 1 Oct restore pay equity law and fund settlement for ~65,000 care and support workers; 3-4 Oct rail and public transport plan.
- Other: $8bn over four years in green policies incl. a Ministry for Green Works (Wikipedia Greens page, above); revoke coal, gold and seabed mining consents under Fast-track Approvals Act 2024.

## 1. Health & Mental Health
- Policy text: "universal, free and accessible" diagnosis and treatment including fully funded public dental and GP clinics (https://www.greens.org.nz/health_policy). Funding amounts, timelines: UNVERIFIED (not on page).
- Mental health: backs the Mental Health and Wellbeing Commission; free mental health services (same page).
- Pharmac: not named; commits to timely access to medicines, diagnostics, gender-affirming and rare-disorder treatments.
- Workforce: fair wages, pay parity, reasonable workloads. Care/support worker pay equity restoration for ~65,000 workers (https://www.greens.org.nz/news).
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED (none retrieved).

## 2. Education
- Fully funded free ECE for all preschool-aged children; fees-free tertiary; write off all student loan debt (https://www.greens.org.nz/education_policy).
- Expand Ka Ora, Ka Ako school lunches to 400 additional schools / 150,000 more children (https://www.greens.org.nz/affordable_food_for_all_of_us).
- Teacher pay, NCEA, student allowance: not found - UNVERIFIED. Manifesto: education as public good (https://www.greens.org.nz/manifesto_2026).
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Crime & Justice
- Judicial discretion: abolish mandatory minimums, life sentences, preventive detention, sentence-discount limits and restrictions on home detention (https://www.greens.org.nz/justice_policy).
- Replace youth justice residences and boot camps with whanau/community rehabilitation; fund restorative and transformative justice incl. Rangatahi and Matariki Courts (same).
- Police and gang policy: nothing found - UNVERIFIED. Prison reform: "transformative and restorative rather than punitive", no specifics.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 4. Housing & Rental Costs
- 40,000 new social homes over five years; cap annual rent increases at 2% (Wikipedia 2026 election page; official figure UNVERIFIED on greens.org.nz).
- "Massive public housing project" run by government; right to housing in legislation; Ministry for Green Works; affordable housing requirements for developers (https://www.greens.org.nz/housing_policy).
- Solar on more than half of public homes within four years; $80m renewable energy for Māori housing (https://www.greens.org.nz/power_for_all_of_us).
- Tax: reverse landlord tax cuts and bright-line changes (https://www.greens.org.nz/tax_system_for_all).
- Rental WoF, Kainga Ora, RMA position: UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Cost of Living & Economy
- Wealth tax 2.5% on net assets above $10m, family home exempt (https://www.greens.org.nz/tax_system_for_all).
- Capital acquisitions (inheritance/gift) tax on assets/gifts over $1m; family farms and homes exempt (same).
- Income tax: new tax-free threshold on first $10,000; higher rate on income over $160,000; party claims 96% get a tax cut (same).
- Corporate: 33% rate for largest corporations (~0.7% of firms); bank levy 0.06% on liabilities of four major banks; 5% withholding tax on offshore tech profits; also private jet tax and higher mining royalties (same; Wikipedia).
- Food: KiwiMart publicly owned supermarket; Woolworths and Foodstuffs to divest at least 120 stores plus distribution; $150m Fair Food Fund; food bank funding $8m to $25m (https://www.greens.org.nz/affordable_food_for_all_of_us).
- Energy: Kiwipower public generator; zero-interest clean energy loans up to 90%; $200m community energy (https://www.greens.org.nz/power_for_all_of_us).
- Income Guarantee: legal right to a minimum income; dollar amount UNVERIFIED (https://www.greens.org.nz/livelihoods_policy).
- Debt/fiscal rule: UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: Wikipedia reports the Greens' tax costings were accidentally released early and later needed an $800m correction (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election) - the underlying outlet report and independent critique (Treasury, NZIER, Tax Working Group, economists on wealth-tax avoidance/revenue) UNVERIFIED.

- Restore pay equity law (https://www.greens.org.nz/news).
- Minimum/living wage figures and Fair Pay Agreements: UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 7. Transport & Infrastructure
- Free public transport for children, teens, students, apprentices, Community Services Card and SuperGold holders; $2 journey caps for everyone else (https://www.greens.org.nz/connect_aotearoa).
- Rail: Wellington-Auckland overnight train pilot; Te Huia extension to Tauranga; restore Southerner Christchurch-Dunedin; Wellington rail renewal (same).
- Roads/urban: bus lanes in three major cities; work toward mass rapid transit; Auckland level crossing removals ($2.5-3.5bn per Wikipedia); more walking/cycling infrastructure.
- EV policy: none on the transport page - UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED.

## 8. Te Tiriti o Waitangi & Māori Issues
- Opposes weakening Tiriti references; calls the government's changes a "Stealth Treaty Principles Bill" (stripping te Tiriti from seven acts, weaker obligations in 19) (https://www.greens.org.nz/te_tiriti_o_waitangi). Stance on the actual Treaty Principles Bill (defeated 2025): UNVERIFIED on page, but consistent with opposition.
- $200m for te reo Māori; Te Waonui a Tane marae funding policy (same; https://www.greens.org.nz/news).
- Marine: embed tikanga, recognise Māori authority, rahui; ban bottom trawling (starting with seamounts), seabed mining ban (https://www.greens.org.nz/waterforlife).
- Co-governance, Whanau Ora, Maori health detail: UNVERIFIED.
- Independent assessment: Waitangi Tribunal found Crown breached Tiriti obligations on education reforms (as stated by the party; primary source UNVERIFIED).

## Update 6 Oct 2026 (tax policy PDF and news feed retrieved)
Source: GreensTaxPolicy2026.pdf (greens.org.nz), greens.org.nz/news, pages supplied by the project owner.
- **Tax costings (Parliamentary Library models, with a 28.5% avoidance/evasion haircut on the wealth tax "based on advice from the Parliamentary Library and informed by Treasury advice"):** super-rich tax 2.5% on net assets above $10m individual / $20m couple (family home, Māori land and post-settlement entities exempt; trusts attributed) $3.76b in 2027/28 rising to $4.14b; Capital Acquisitions Tax 33% on inheritances/gifts over $1m (about 1,100 people a year) $0.95b-$1.10b; corporate rate 33% for firms with turnover over $30m (0.7% of firms) $1.37b-$1.57b; bank levy 0.06% on banks with over $100b liabilities $0.37b-$0.42b; big tech withholding enforcement $0.2b; reversing landlord interest deductibility $0.88b-$1.0b and the bright-line test (back to 10 years) $45m; income tax changes (tax-free $10,000, then 10%, 17.5%, 25.5%, 30.5%, 33.5%, and 45% over $160,000) cost $2.3b-$2.7b; IRD funding $100m. Net total +$5.15b (2027/28) to +$5.73b (2030/31). Behavioural responses other than wealth-tax avoidance are not modelled.
- **News feed Aug-Oct 2026:** transport plan (4 Oct); intercity passenger rail (3 Oct); Ipsos survey cited for wealth-tax support (25 Sep); $200m te reo Māori (14 Sep); Te Waonui a Tāne marae funding (9 Aug); pay equity restoration (1 Oct); city-centre vacant-lease auctions (20 Sep). No housing announcement specifying 40,000 homes or a 2% rent cap appears in the feed or the housing policy page; those figures remain Wikipedia-sourced and UNVERIFIED.
- **Coalition:** Wikipedia (citing a Waitangi press conference, early Feb 2026) records Labour and the Greens committing to work together during the election and in government.
- Pass-2 crawl: Infometrics (Brad Olsen) worked on and reviewed the Greens' tax costings; the $800m error (June 2026) was IRD administration funding of ~$100m a year recorded as revenue instead of cost, corrected within a day; net revenue became $5.147b (2027/28) rising to $5.725b (RNZ 22 Jun 2026; Newswire). Transport plan (30 Sep-4 Oct 2026): free fares for children, students, apprentices, Community Services Card and Total Mobility users, $2 cap for others, mass rapid transit in three cities, overnight Auckland-Wellington rail, Southerner, Te Huia to Tauranga, Western Line level-crossing removals; total about $24b including $21b for mass transit and rail; wealth tax cited as funding, fiscal plan to come (The Spinoff, 30 Sep 2026). MATA-Horizon Maori-roll poll: Greens 28% lead the party vote.
- Pass-2 crawl: housing policy confirmed by RNZ, NZ Herald and The Post: Renters' Rights Bill capping rent increases at 2% a year, ending no-cause evictions, a rental warrant of fitness and a national landlord register; 'tens of thousands' of new public homes; Kainga Ora and CHP funding for accessible homes; Whai Kainga Whai Oranga scaled up; costed at nearly $14b over five years (RNZ). The '40,000 homes' figure is not in these reports.
