# Te Pāti Māori - 2026 Election Policy Research

Research date: 2026-10-06. Election: 7 November 2026.

## Method and limitations (read first)
- WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before this task started, so NO news-outlet searches were possible. Evidence comes only from WebFetch of the party's own site (maoriparty.org.nz) and Wikipedia pages (which cite news outlets; the underlying articles were NOT opened).
- The party's policy pages are short summaries; detailed numbers sit in linked Google Drive PDFs that were NOT retrieved. Most domain items therefore have no numbers. Where a figure comes from Wikipedia only, it is marked (Wikipedia-sourced; primary article unverified).
- Items not found are marked UNVERIFIED. No independent assessments (Treasury, Waitangi Tribunal, MoJ, etc.) could be fetched; those are listed as UNVERIFIED background pointers only, not findings.
- Some fetches (te_reo_maori, mokopuna_m_ori, freshwater_policy) returned HTTP 429 and were not read.

## Update 6 Oct 2026 (full manifesto PDFs retrieved; supersedes summaries above)
Source: Te Pāti Māori Policy Manifesto 2026 chapters linked from maoriparty.org.nz (Google Drive PDFs for health, justice, tax, Te Tiriti entrenchment, housing, cost of living). Page text supplied by the project owner and PDFs downloaded 6 Oct 2026.
- **Health:** Māori Health Commissioning Agency; transfer 25% of total health and ACC funding to Māori-led systems; Māori Accident Compensation Authority; binding Te Tiriti clause in health law; Te Tiriti Hauora Commissioner; free primary healthcare and free dental care for all whānau; free medication delivery; marae-based clinics; bowel screening from 45 (35 for Māori and Pacific); $500m a year for kaupapa Māori mental health; $1b a year for workforce development; increase Pharmac funding; Māori Data Authority; alcohol marketing ban; restore smokefree targets.
- **Justice:** Independent Māori Justice Authority funded by reallocating 50% of Vote Corrections, 50% of Vote Police and 50% of Vote Courts; repeal Three Strikes, the Gangs Act, the Sentencing (Reform) Amendment Act and the Bail Amendment Act; raise the age of criminal responsibility from 10 to 16; decriminalise drug use and wipe convictions; reinstate cultural reports; body cameras; close military-style academies immediately and youth justice residences by 2030; restore prisoner voting; voting age 16; abolish prisons by 2040 (confirmed, not just reported).
- **Tax (modelled on government data, "benchmarked against Treasury and Parliamentary Service analysis"):** income tax 0% to $30,000, 15% to $60,000, 33% to $90,000, 39% to $180,000, 42% to $300,000, 48% above ($13.9b cost); Income Tax Kai Credit worth eight weeks of food for incomes under $60,000 ($2.8b); net wealth tax 1.5% $2m-$5m, 2% $5m-$10m, 2.5% above $10m ($8.8b); company tax back to 33% ($3.4b); 5% international profit transfer tax ($1.0b); land-banking tax 33% ($2.8b); vacant house tax 2% ($1.7b); stamp duty 5% on residential sales, first-home buyers under $1m exempt ($2.1b); $1b for IRD/SFO/FMA enforcement raising $4.1b. Party claims 97% get a tax cut averaging $4,000.
- **Te Tiriti entrenchment:** Mātike Mai Fund $220m over four years (not $200m); 2040 target for constitutional transformation; UNDRIP national action plan within 100 days; independent Te Tiriti Commission as an Officer of Parliament with power to investigate, require remedial action and issue Te Tiriti Compliance Orders that suspend executive decisions (Crown must apply to the High Court to overturn); Waitangi Tribunal recommendations made binding via amendment to the Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975. A Parliamentary Commissioner for Te Tiriti was named a coalition bottom line in February 2025.
- **Housing:** 2,000 homes on ancestral Māori land within four years; boost Kāinga Ora building and stop state-house sales; 50% of new social housing allocated to Māori; all new government builds accessible; end sales of freehold land to offshore interests (leasehold only); 2% vacant house tax; repeal interest deductibility (redistribute the ~$3b); universal rent control (freeze, then CPI-linked increases); repeal no-cause evictions in 100 days; Rangatahi Ora youth homelessness agency with a legal duty to assist.
- **Cost of living and employment:** minimum wage immediately to the living wage ($28.95) and indexed; abolish starting-out and youth rates; restore pay equity; protect union access and multi-employer bargaining; double baseline benefits (upper WEAG range); remove sanctions and work tests; individualise benefits; cancel income-support debt; free public transport for children, students and Community Services Card holders immediately, half-price for others, free for everyone within five years; write off the living-cost component of student loans; universal student allowance at double current rates.
- **Strategy:** one-person party list (Lance Norman) announced 4 Oct 2026; supporters asked to give TPM the candidate vote and Labour or the Greens the party vote, explicitly to create an overhang (RNZ, 4 Oct 2026). Labour's Willie Jackson warned this risks a backlash against the Māori seats.
