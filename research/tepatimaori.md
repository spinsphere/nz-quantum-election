# Te Pāti Māori - 2026 Election Policy Research

Research date: 2026-10-06. Election: 7 November 2026.

## Method and limitations (read first)
- WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before this task started, so NO news-outlet searches were possible. Evidence comes only from WebFetch of the party's own site (maoriparty.org.nz) and Wikipedia pages (which cite news outlets; the underlying articles were NOT opened).
- The party's policy pages are short summaries; detailed numbers sit in linked Google Drive PDFs that were NOT retrieved. Most domain items therefore have no numbers. Where a figure comes from Wikipedia only, it is marked (Wikipedia-sourced; primary article unverified).
- Items not found are marked UNVERIFIED. No independent assessments (Treasury, Waitangi Tribunal, MoJ, etc.) could be fetched; those are listed as UNVERIFIED background pointers only, not findings.
- Some fetches (te_reo_maori, mokopuna_m_ori, freshwater_policy) returned HTTP 429 and were not read.

## Party status, leadership and stability
- Co-leaders: Rawiri Waititi (Waiariki) and Debbie Ngarewa-Packer (Te Tai Hauāuru). Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election
- President: John Tamihere; he donated $60,000 in 2026 (donations to 24 Sep 2026: $114,000 from two donors; electoral funding total $255,986.50). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election
- Seats: 4 of 123 (down from 6 won in 2023). Wikipedia lists Waiariki (Waititi), Te Tai Hauāuru (Ngarewa-Packer), Tāmaki Makaurau (Oriini Kaipara, won in a September 2025 by-election), plus a fourth seat (Hana-Rawhiti Maipi-Clarke, Hauraki-Waikato, named in the suspension list; electorate list in the fetched summary was inconsistent, so exact current seat-holder list is UNVERIFIED). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_P%C4%81ti_M%C4%81ori
- May 2025: Privileges Committee suspensions for the haka during the Treaty Principles Bill vote (Nov 2024): Maipi-Clarke 7 days; Waititi and Ngarewa-Packer 21 days each. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_P%C4%81ti_M%C4%81ori
- Mariameno Kapa-Kingi (Te Tai Tokerau MP): suspended 23 Oct 2025, expelled by national council 9 Nov 2025; interim injunction reinstating her 5 Dec 2025 (Justice Radich); 10 Mar 2026 High Court found suspension/expulsion unlawful (breach of party kawa). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariameno_Kapa-Kingi
- Tākuta Ferris (Te Tai Tonga): expelled alongside Kapa-Kingi in Nov 2025; current status "unclear/contested" per Wikipedia. UNVERIFIED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_P%C4%81ti_M%C4%81ori
- Split: Kapa-Kingi left and founded the Te Tai Tokerau Party on 11 May 2026; registered with the Electoral Commission 5 Aug 2026; about 200 dues-paying members initially; undecided on running beyond Te Tai Tokerau; open to post-election cooperation with any party incl. National; set conditions for return to TPM (leadership change, public apologies). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Tai_Tokerau_Party
- TPM's Te Tai Tokerau candidate: Aperahama Edwards (Ngātiwai Trust Board chair), announced 27 May 2026. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_P%C4%81ti_M%C4%81ori
- Party website shows candidates/electorate tiles: Waititi, Ngarewa-Packer, Oriini Kaipara (Tāmaki Makaurau), plus Ikaroa-Rāwhiti, Hauraki-Waikato, Te Tai Tonga, Te Tai Tokerau (names not captured). Other 2026 electorate candidates UNVERIFIED. Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz
- Labour (Hipkins) will contest all seven Māori electorates, citing TPM internal conflict. Labour and Greens announced in Feb 2026 they would work together in election and government. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election
- TPM's own coalition statements about Labour: UNVERIFIED (not found).
- Māori roll 302,096 vs general-roll Māori 255,627; overseas enrolments in Māori electorates up 67% since 2023. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_New_Zealand_general_election
- Polling for TPM: UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Cost of Living & Economy
- Cost of living: lift incomes to "live with dignity"; strengthen welfare; back practical cost-of-living relief including transport affordability. Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz/cost_of_living
- Tax (page): wealthiest pay fair share; revenue funds housing, health, education, whānau support. Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz/tax
- Tax (late Aug 2026, Wikipedia-sourced): 48% rate on income above NZ$300,001; rebate for those earning below NZ$30,000; wealth, company, land-banking and vacant-home taxes; stamp duty. GST-off-food and foreign company tax specifics: UNVERIFIED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_P%C4%81ti_M%C4%81ori
- Energy Sovereignty: $1 billion Energy Sovereignty Fund; National Independence Energy Plan; Māori energy workforce and industry; rural energy capability; regulate energy system in the public interest. Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz/energy_sovereignty
- Kai Sovereignty: protect Māori food systems and break corporate control (grocery specifics UNVERIFIED). Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz
- Economic development: Māori-led institutions and funds for Māori enterprise; trade, innovation, wealth building; Māori data sovereignty. Source: https://www.maoriparty.org.nz/economic_development
- Independent assessment: UNVERIFIED (no Treasury/IRD costing of the 48% rate or wealth tax found).
