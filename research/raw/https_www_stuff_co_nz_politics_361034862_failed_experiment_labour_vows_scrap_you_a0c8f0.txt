‘Failed experiment’: Labour vows to scrap youth offender boot camps

Labour has announced it will scrap the Government’s youth offender boot camps if elected.

The Military Style Academy was one of the coalition Government’s flagship law and order policies, which National campaigned on ahead of the 2023 election.

The boot camps began in 2024 at a specifically refurbished Oranga Tamariki youth justice centre in Palmerston North.

In a statement on Thursday, Labour’s children’s spokesperson Willow-Jean Prime said the boot camps were a “failed experiment” which were “taking us back down a road of harm”.

The Military Style Academy is based at Te Au Rere a te Tonga, a youth justice facility in Palmerston North.

Prime has previously referred to the Royal Commission of Inquiry report into abuse in care, which revealed serious abuses at previous boot camp programmes.

“Christopher Luxon has gone against decades of evidence, survivor testimonies, and the abysmal results of his own failed pilots to turn boot camps into law,” she said.

“We saw it in the first pilot, where the majority of participants went on to reoffend. We saw it in the second pilot, where two alleged assaults occurred and two participants voluntarily left.

“This is all happening against a backdrop of serious incidents in our youth justice facilities and growing concerns about abuse in state care.”

Oranga Tamariki and Minister for Children Karen Chhour hailed the initial boot camp pilot a success, despite 80% of its participants going on to reoffend within a year of their release.

An 80% reoffending rate is also the average for the rest of the youth justice system.

A review of the boot camp programme noted that the reoffending was less severe than would typically be expected if they’d gone through alternative youth justice programmes.

Prime said people in the youth justice system required rehabilitation, therapeutic support and access to services, rather than “a bumper sticker slogan that sends them back out worse”.

Children's Commissioner Jane Searle said military-style boot camps disproportionally affected tamariki Māori and children with complex needs. Photo: David Unwin / The Post

“Labour will scrap National’s boot camps and focus on evidence-based interventions that help young people turn their lives around.”

Children’s Commissioner Jane Searle said military-style academies were not the answer to youth offending.

“You cannot march young people into good behaviour.”

She added that military-style boot camps disproportionately affected children and young people already over-represented in the youth justice system, such as Māori and those with complex needs and neurodisabilities.

“Almost all mokopuna who come into contact with the youth justice system have experienced significant trauma, adversity, abuse and neglect.

“What I do support is investment in evidence-based, therapeutic, trauma-informed rehabilitation.”
