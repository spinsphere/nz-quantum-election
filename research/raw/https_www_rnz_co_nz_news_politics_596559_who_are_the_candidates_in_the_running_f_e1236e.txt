Who are the candidates in the running for Te Tai Tokerau?

Race heats up in Te Tai Tokerau electorate
Morning Report
28 May 2026
Queue

The race for Te Tai Tokerau is on, with Te Pāti Māori confirming its candidate in the hotly contested seat.

Aperahama Edwards joins Labour's Willow-Jean Prime, the Greens Hūhana Lyndon and newly formed Te Tai Tokerau party's Mariameno Kapa-Kingi - who recently split from Te Pāti Māori after being expelled then reinstated.

It makes for a tight race, just five months out from the election.

RNZ spoke to the candidates following Te Pāti Māori's announcement.

Te Pāti Māori

Aperahama Edwards.

RNZ / Cole Eastham-Farrelly

Announced on Wednesday, Aperahama Edwards - of Ngātiwai descent and chair of the Ngātiwai Trust Board - said he was standing because "our people need a voice that will not bend when Te Tiriti is under attack", he said.

"I have a team that's ready to go in response to the call from our people to stand, and with a deep conviction and belief in the foundations of what Te Pāti Māori stands for, which is a party that centres Māori."

Edwards said he had served Te Tai Tokerau for many years through hapū, iwi and the community, and represented the region nationally and internationally, he said.

"Always at the heart of it is my deep-rooted connection to Te Tai Tokerau ... that's my 'why'."

He acknowledged his fellow candidates' leadership and mana, but backed his chances to take the seat.

"They've had three years at this, and looking at the time ahead, I've got barely five months, so I understand that the struggle will be real. But we're prepared for it, and it's not as if I'm starting from nowhere," he said.

"I have been serving our people for a long time ... we'll be building off that."

He recognised Te Pāti Māori's internal turmoil which resulted in the expulsion of Kapa-Kingi (before her eventual reinstatement, followed by her decision to leave the party) and Tākuta Ferris - who will stand as an independent for Te Tai Tonga.

"I acknowledge what's transpired, and those that have been impacted by it. My focus is the aspirations and hopes of Te Tai Tokerau," he said.

Mariameno Kapa-Kingi.

RNZ / Samuel Rillstone

Te Tai Tokerau

Having struck out on her own following tensions with Te Pāti Māori's leadership, Mariameno Kapa-Kingi said she was surprised at Edwards' decision to "support the current leadership".

"That's says something to me," she said, but acknowledged it was up to him, "Kei ā ia te tikanga."

She said she was focused on her plans for Te Tai Tokerau, "Which I'm terribly excited about."

"The race is on," she acknowledged.

Having secured enough paying members to formalise her new party, she felt "encouraged and uplifted".

Kapa-Kingi said her experience of around 40 years "in the field" across the region, and her work to advocate for whānau would help her in the contest.

"I'm mature enough, grunty enough. I've got backbone, and I've got a reputation of working on the ground across Tai Tokerau, and yeah, I know that's what I'm going to take to the campaign."

Her approach of having local decision making and a focus on mana mokopuna and tino rangatiratanga were what she would speak to as the candidate.

She pointed to the office she shared with independent MP Tākuta Ferris, who holds the Te Tai Tonga seat.

"This office is shared by Tai Tonga and Tai Tokerau right? The tail and the head. We work together, we caucus together, and we vote together."

She described it as having an independent as well as an interdependent model of working, "We are demonstrating that right here, right now. So, imagine if that was across the motu.

"We're a tiny little block of two, right now, voting together, but imagine what's possible if other electorates were to see the opportunity in it as well."

Hūhana Lyndon.

RNZ / Peter de Graaf

The Green Party

Hūhana Lyndon said having Edwards in the mix brough an "additional dynamic" to the race. She wished him well, but said it was a competition.

She said people needed to vote for an electorate MP who they want to "do the mahi", and bring their voice and their concerns back into Parliament, so "I'm that person."

The Greens have not won in the Māori seats yet, but Lyndon said she was feeling energised and confident, "having done an immense amount of work over the last term".

"I know that a lot of people appreciate the way that I follow through and have been doing a lot of work in the community that, as a list MP, is a bit above and beyond, but that's a part of my kaupapa to deliver outcomes for our people."

Her plan to win was to continue showing up and listening. There were many issues within the kainga (home) she said, but solutions were also from the kainga, and needed to be supported by the Crown.

She acknowledged key issues were jobs, housing and sustainable infrastructure, as well as pointing to the massive pressures on a tired health workforce. She also said there was limited access to health services in the north.

She dismissed the notion Māori voters needed to be strategic, encouraging instead for them to vote for an electorate MP who was going to be seen, have workers on the ground, and the doors open for people.

"The electorate MP needs to be focused on what's important for the community, but then a party who can work and strive and advance those things that are important for Māori.

"We're absolutely confident with what we have to offer in a Green government."

Willow-Jean Prime.

RNZ / Angus Dreaver

The Labour Party

The Labour party lost six of the seven Māori seats at the last election, and Māori campaign chair Willie Jackson was making it known the party wants to win them back.

Willow-Jean Prime was running for the first time in a Māori seat, and spoke about previously working closely with former Te Tai Tokerau MP Kelvin Davis, "delivering for our people".

"To be able to have the opportunity to put my name forward and to seek support to have that honour and privilege of representing Te Tai Tokerau as their member of Parliament and in government would just be wonderful."

Following Edwards' announcement she said, "It's going to be a very interesting election in Te Tai Tokerau, as everybody knows."

She was excited about the campaign.

"I am offering somebody who is experienced, who is stable, and who is focused on the issues that matter most to Te Tai Tokerau."

Acknowledging the range of candidates, Prime said everybody brought their own strengths.

"I am standing on my track record. I bring experience. I have been a member of Parliament for almost nine years now, I've been a cabinet minister. I hold the portfolios of social development and employment, children, and Maori education, and I bring that experience and stable hand to politics and to Parliament."

She said people were most concerned about jobs, health, housing, the cost of living, youth unemployment, and the prospects for young people.

It would be for voters to decide who their electorate MP should be, she said, "I don't think anybody should take the voters for granted and assume anything.

"Absolutely, the race is on."
