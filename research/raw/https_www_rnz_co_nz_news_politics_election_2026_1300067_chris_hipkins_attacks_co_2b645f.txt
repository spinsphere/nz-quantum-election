Chris Hipkins attacks coalition on Paris Agreement
Russell Palmer
Political reporter
Caption:
Labour's Chris Hipkins.
Photo credit:
RNZ / Baz Macdonald

Labour's leader Chris Hipkins is lumping National in with ACT and New Zealand First's promises to exit the Paris Agreement, after weeks of National doing the same with Labour and tax.

Hipkins on Tuesday morning announced a new policy alongside his climate spokesperson Deborah Russell: an urgent review to fix the Emissions Trading Scheme (ETS).

Hipkins said the carbon price was too low to drive meaningful emissions reductions, and make it cheaper to plant exotic trees than grow food.

He said the review would identify changes, reconsider the role of forestry, and develop rules to prevent whole-farm conversions.

New ETS forestry registrations would be paused while the review was being done.

ACT and NZ First have both promised to exit the Paris agreement, but National's leader Christopher Luxon ruled that out last week.

ACT has also proposed abolishing the Climate Commission.

Hipkins said Labour was fully committed to the Paris agreement and would put New Zealand on course to meet its targets.

"The National Party and their mates want to pull us out of the Paris Agreement. They want to abolish the Climate Commission. They want to wind back all action on climate change," he said.

"There are ministers in this government that don't even believe that climate change is real. We cannot afford three more years of this government."

National and its MPs have been running advertising and social media promos for weeks accusing Labour "and their mates" of wanting to bring in at least nine new taxes - despite Hipkins having ruled out taxes other than the narrow capital gains tax the party is campaigning on.

Hipkins has repeatedly called the adverts lies, but his coalition partners have maintained that Labour cannot simply rule out their ideas ahead of negotiations to form a government.

National's Christopher Luxon has said remaining in the Paris climate agreement would be a bottom line in coalition negotiations.

RNZ / Nick Monro

Now, he appears to be turning that same argument on National and Luxon's climate promises.

"The National Party basically works in government with NZ First and ACT, both of whom have been clear they want to pull New Zealand out of the Paris Agreement. That means the National Party can't say that [they won't]," he said.

He could not remember which ministers did not believe in climate change, but said he could find examples.

"You've seen them on the other side, you know, talking about that. I mean, I can find you the various quotes. I haven't got them off the top of my head.

"They want to abandon action on climate change in New Zealand. Winston Peters has been very clear on that. David Seymour has been very clear on that."

Successive governments have ignored Climate Commission recommendations about how to improve the scheme across multiple reviews.

Hipkins said a review was needed because changes to the ETS needed to be worked through carefully, "recognising that there are billions of dollars invested through the ETS at the moment, so the responsible thing to do is to commit to a rapid review".

"The Climate Commission's evidence base that they and the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment's evidence base will certainly inform that review."

The NZ Herald on Sunday reported Hipkins had not committed to agricultural emissions pricing - despite Labour having pledged to do so at every election since 2008 - after National wound down the policy.

Hipkins took that a step further on Tuesday, saying Labour was "not going to be pricing agriculture by default".

"We want to reduce agricultural emissions. We think that we can work with farmers to do that without putting a price on methane emissions."

Major exporters like Fonterra and Silver Fern Farms were driving some change on emissions reductions themselves, he said, and new technology could make the on-farm economics work.

"I've visited farms where they're reducing their irrigation and they're reducing their use of synthetic fertiliser because it's good economics for them. New technology allows them to do that more precisely, and that means that they're reducing their emissions in a way that actually helps them with their farm balance sheet.

"It's going to be a good cost of living policy as well. Renewable energy is cheaper than fossil fuel energy. Good climate policy, good economic policy."

He said farmers knew that if they did not meet climate commitments including under the EU free trade agreement their access to international markets would be compromised.

Asked how Labour would meet the expected cost of falling short on climate targets, he said accelerating emissions reductions by fixing the ETS would help with increasing renewable energy and EV uptake.

Reducing emissions in that way would in turn would bring the cost down.

"There is a lot of work underway that's been sitting dormant for three years that we can get underway again," he said, and would aim to do so by 2030 when the next target is due.

"We'd certainly give it a very good go".

National strikes back

National's climate spokesperson Simon Watts soon pushed back, saying Labour's policy could end up costing New Zealand households more than $400 extra a year.

"Aiming to drive up the carbon price will add costs to households, through higher electricity bills, more expensive petrol, and higher costs to get food from farms to your home," he said.

National climate spokesperson Simon Watts

RNZ/Marika Khabazi

He pointed to an official information release from the Ministry for the Environment which indicated an increase of $10 for New Zealand Units under the ETS would add about $87 for the average household.

"Labour is already campaigning on a capital gains tax, a $5 billion tax on businesses and a bed tax. Their mates want an inheritance tax, a higher top tax rate, a tax on rentals, a gift tax, and a tax on land right across the country - and now they want to whack Kiwi households by increasing the price of carbon.

"National has a sensible plan to reduce emissions while still growing the economy, creating jobs and lift incomes - working with businesses and using new technology to reduce carbon without pulling a massive economic handbrake."

Speaking at the 18th Climate Change and Business Conference in Auckland where Hipkins announced Labour's new policy, Watts said ETS units were "private property rights" owned by foresters and farmers and other major sector players.

"So I'm not going to sit up here sort of throwing around ideas which are going to, in effect, devalue that market, or on the flip side, have implications around massive increase in supply of that market," he said.

"We are very clear that we need the ETS to do the heavy lifting in regards to emissions reduction. We need credible policy in regards to that. We have introduced limits on exotic forestry into the ETS. No government before us have done that."

He said he was continuing to speak to the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment about other changes that were needed, but "there's no policy decisions in regards to that at this point".

Documents released last month showed the coalition had considered cancelling some carbon auctions altogether after its policy changes tanked the carbon price, but opted to stick with the status quo after officials warned doing so could cause further uncertainty.

Hipkins 'flailing' - Luxon

The prime minister was later asked about having ministers that did not believe in climate change, to which he responded "what a load of rubbish", before listing a range of actions by the government on climate change.

"This is the Government that has delivered the lowest quarterly emissions out of this country since records began in 2010."

He then took a swipe at the Labour leader, saying Hipkins was "just flailing at the moment".

"Like he honestly is, he's honestly flailing.

"If he wants to be prime minister of New Zealand, I'm sorry, this far out from an election, he's gotta show up [with some] solutions."

'Paris has to change' - ACT

Asked if it was still ACT's position to leave the Paris Climate Accords, party leader David Seymour said their position was more nuanced than that.

"We say Paris has to change or we have to leave," he said.

"New Zealand is an agricultural economy, half our emissions are methane, methane is scientifically very different from carbon dioxide, we need to negotiate a better deal that reflects our reality."

Seymour said it was critical to be wary of trade retaliation from foreign customers and governments if the country were to leave the agreement.

"On the other hand, it's equally important that if the rest of the world is not pulling its weight that we don't keep being goodie-two-shoes and get put out of business," he said.

Speaking on Labour's plans to review the Emissions Trading Scheme , Seymour said he would make sure the scheme had a reasonable cap.
