Watch live: Labour and Greens speak from Waitangi Treaty Grounds
Caption:
The Greens and Labour leadership on Tuesday.

Labour and the Greens say they have shown they can work together without insults - attempting to contrast themselves with the coalition - despite the conspicuous absence of Te Pāti Māori.

The leaders of the two parties held a joint media conference together at the Waitangi Treaty Grounds in a show of unity.

Neither party unveiled new policy.

Labour's leader Chris Hipkins began by saying they were sending a clear message that unity was important.

"We're stronger as a country when we are unified and when we work constructively together," he said. "We also want to be able to model the fact that you can have your disagreements, still respect each other, you don't need to insult each other every time you disagree.

"I think that is a stark contrast with what we're seeing from the current government, where you've got Winston Peters, Christopher Luxon, David Seymour taking pot shots at each other every time they disagree with each other. That's not good for the country. It's not good for New Zealanders' overall sense of national unity."

Green co-leader Marama Davidson said the party had seen examples in Waitangi showing that "Te Tiriti really is unity".

"Te Tiriti requires leadership to step up and take the guidance of the communities who especially over the past term have had to mobilise against this government's attacks on Te Tiriti, attacks on te ao Māori, attacks on our connections as tangata whenua and tangata tiriti."

No poll this term has shown Labour and the Greens able to form a government without support from Te Pāti Māori, which has been riven by disputes between its leadership and some of its MPs.

MP for Te Tai Tokerau Mariameno Kapa-Kingi and party president John Tamihere both attended a court hearing on Monday to resolve an ongoing dispute about Kapa-Kingi's expulsion from the party alongside Te Tai Tonga MP Tākuta Ferris in November.

Asked about that party's absence from the standup, Hipkins said Labour and the Greens had "a different working relationship" with Te Pāti Māori, which needed to resolve its internal issues "before they can sit down and talk to other people".

Davidson said she had acknowledged "this whole time through the troubles that ae, Te Pāti Māori have things to work out, absolutely, but we have continued to stay in contact with them through all of the troubles, and ... we hope they work things out. We've got a big job to do, which is get rid of this anti-Māori, anti-Tiriti government".

Both leaders pushed back on questions about polls, Hipkins saying it was "premature" with months of campaigning to go, and Davidson saying people could "take the power into their hands and be the polls".

Hipkins confirmed he had seen Te Pāti Māori co-leader Rawiri Waititi when both had travelled to Mount Maunganui to see the damage from the deadly slip, but said they had not spoken about the party's internal division.

In an earlier statement, Finance Minister and National Party deputy Nicola Willis characterised Hipkins' promise to release a fiscal plan at a later date as having "repeatedly refused to rule out expanding his Capital Gains Tax".

"Adding to that, this week Labour is set to signal its willingness to share power with the most radical iteration of the Green Party ever, opening the door to the $88.8 billion of extra taxes the Greens are promising to introduce on top of Labour's CGT," Willis said.

She said anything short of Hipkins ruling out expanding the CGT, taking on the Greens' inheritance tax and the wealth tax, and increases to company tax, personal tax and mining taxes would "make clear that everything is on the table".

Hipkins' response to those statements was withering.

"I'm amazed that Nicola Willis has so much time on her hands. You would think that maybe she would be focused on balancing the government's books, something that she hasn't yet done and has no credible plan to do," he said.

Earlier, Hipkins told Morning Report the joint media conference was intended to demonstrate an ability to cooperate sensibly in any future government, which was not the case within the coalition.

He had pointed to NZ First leader Winston Peters' staunch opposition to the India free trade agreement - which Labour is still considering whether or not to support.

"When you've got the Foreign Affairs Minister saying the Prime Minister's not telling the truth, I think that's an indictment on the current government."

Hipkins said the left parties had strong areas of agreement too, such as their refusal to advance any policy like the Treaty Principles Bill.
